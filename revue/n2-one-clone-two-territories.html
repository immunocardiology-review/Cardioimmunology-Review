Abstract

Clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP) is the age-related expansion of a blood clone carrying somatic mutations, without hematologic malignancy; it nearly doubles coronary risk. Two studies published in 2026 find it on both sides of the patient with cancer. Among 10,571 patients with solid tumors, cells from the clone infiltrated the tumor in 18%, with poorer survival. Among 8,004 patients treated for a solid tumor, CHIP was associated with more heart failure at ten years. The same clone inhabits the tumor and the vessel. We propose reading clonal hematopoiesis as an interface, and cardio-oncology as the medicine of a single patient.

Introduction

With age, hematopoietic stem cells acquire somatic mutations, and one of them eventually supplies a measurable share of circulating leukocytes. This clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP) affects 9.5% of people aged 70 to 79 years and 18.4% of those older than 90 years.1 It increases the risk of hematologic cancer, but also that of coronary heart disease and ischemic stroke.1,2

Two studies published in 2026 in JAMA Oncology examine it in the patient with cancer, each from one side. One looks for it in the tumor,3 the other in the heart after treatment.4 Read together, they describe a single clone in two territories. This is the reading of cardioimmunology, which studies the contribution of immune mechanisms to cardiovascular disease.5

The Clone, from the Marrow to the Vessel

In prospective cohorts, carriers of CHIP had a risk of coronary heart disease multiplied by 1.9 (95% confidence interval [CI], 1.4 to 2.7) and, before age 50 years, a risk of myocardial infarction multiplied by 4.0 (95% CI, 2.4 to 6.7).2 Mutations in DNMT3A, TET2, ASXL1 and JAK2 each contributed.2

The mechanism was established in mice. Tet2-deficient bone marrow cells are enough to enlarge atherosclerotic plaques; mutant macrophages secrete more interleukin 1β through the NLRP3 inflammasome, and an NLRP3 inhibitor protects animals carrying the clone more.6 The Treatise made it one of its paradigms: cardiovascular risk is also read in the marrow.5

The Clone Enters the Tumor

Among 421 patients with early-stage non–small cell lung cancer enrolled in the TRACERx study, CHIP was present in 34%, and cells from the clone infiltrated the tumor in 42% of carriers.7 This infiltration was associated with a risk of death or recurrence multiplied by 1.80 (95% CI, 1.23 to 2.63) compared with the absence of CHIP. In mice, Tet2-mutant monocytes migrated toward tumor cells, enriched the tumor in myeloid cells and promoted the growth of tumor organoids.7

Yun and colleagues extend this finding to 10,571 patients with solid tumors, using whole-genome sequencing from the Genomics England project.3 Cells from the clone infiltrated the tumor in 1,943 patients (18.4%), and up to 32% in endometrial cancers. This infiltration was more frequent with age and after cytotoxic chemotherapy (odds ratio, 1.24; 95% CI, 1.06 to 1.44). It was associated with poorer overall survival (hazard ratio, 1.13; 95% CI, 1.02 to 1.25), especially in breast cancer (1.95; 95% CI, 1.54 to 2.48).3 The study is retrospective: it establishes an association, not a cause.

Cancer Treatment Chooses the Clone

Among 8,810 patients with nonhematologic cancers, clonal hematopoiesis was present in one in four.8 Among 24,146 patients, radiotherapy, platinum agents and topoisomerase II inhibitors preferentially selected mutations in DNA damage response genes: TP53, PPM1D and CHEK2.9 Treatment does not merely injure cells: it chooses, among them, those that will survive.

After Cancer, the Heart

Shyr and colleagues followed 8,004 patients treated for a solid tumor with chemotherapy, radiotherapy or immunotherapy, in a biobank linked to the medical record and to whole-genome sequencing.4 CHIP, defined by a variant allele fraction of at least 2%, was present in 549 of them (6.9%). In the matched cohort, the 10-year cumulative incidence of heart failure reached 20.3% in carriers vs 14.5%, and that of ischemic cardiovascular disease 25.3% vs 18.5% (figure 1).

After adjustment, the association remained significant for heart failure (subdistribution hazard ratio, 1.26; 95% CI, 1.02 to 1.56), but not for ischemic disease or arrhythmias.4 An interaction with intensive chemotherapy, of at least seven cycles, appears only in an exploratory analysis. A single biobank, a single sequencing effort: the signal needs to be replicated.

The Immunotherapy Paradox

In mice, hematopoietic inactivation of Tet2 improves the response to immune checkpoint inhibitors; among patients with melanoma treated with these inhibitors, TET2 clonal hematopoiesis was associated with a sixfold higher probability of clinical benefit.10 The clone that helps the tumor grow might also help immunotherapy fight it.

On the cardiac side, a study of 88 patients treated with checkpoint inhibitors reports myocarditis in 73% of CHIP carriers vs 41% of noncarriers (hazard ratio, 2.74; 95% CI, 1.44 to 5.22).11 These patients had been referred to cardio-oncology, often for suspected cardiac toxicity, and the diagnosis rested on MRI, without biopsy; the authors acknowledge that this frequency exceeds that of patients treated in the general population.11 The signal remains to be verified in an unselected cohort.

What the Treatise Does Not Yet Say

The Treatise describes clonal hematopoiesis as an immune determinant of coronary risk and heart failure. It notes that, in an exploratory analysis of the CANTOS trial, carriers of a TET2 clone appeared to derive greater benefit from canakinumab (hazard ratio, 0.38; 95% CI, 0.15 to 0.96; P = .14 for interaction).5,12 Its chapter on cardio-oncology, however, does not mention clonal hematopoiesis.5

The American Heart Association scientific statement, published in 2026, notes that no treatment specific to clonal hematopoiesis has proven cardiovascular efficacy.13 The link with solid tumors remains much less well understood than the link with hematologic cancers.14 The proposal that follows is therefore a hypothesis.

Proposal

One Clone, Two Territories

Reading the clone as an interface between the tumor and the vessel, rather than as two separate risks, leads to three questions, each to be tested (table 1). The first is descriptive: to report, in cardio-oncology cohorts, the presence and size of the clone, as one reports age or the treatment received. The second is prognostic: does a clone that infiltrates the tumor also predict heart failure after treatment? Neither study measured it. The third is therapeutic: does targeting interleukin 1β, which protects the vessel of the mouse carrying a Tet2 clone, also modify the tumor and the response to immunotherapy?

Conclusion

The two 2026 studies do not say the same thing, and that is what makes them useful together: one shows the clone in the tumor, the other its weight on the heart after treatment. They invite cardio-oncology to read a single patient, and a single cell in two territories.

Practice Points

  • Clonal hematopoiesis is frequent in patients with cancer, and even more so after radiotherapy or chemotherapy.
  • Testing for it is not, to date, a routine examination in cardio-oncology, and no specific treatment has proven cardiovascular efficacy.
  • When it is known, for example when discovered on blood or tumor sequencing, it calls for careful management of cardiovascular risk factors and monitoring of cardiac function during and after treatment.
  • The myocarditis signal under immunotherapy rests on a selected cohort and should not, on its own, change the decision to give immunotherapy.

These points summarize the cited data; they do not replace medical advice.

Multiple-Choice Question

According to the 2026 studies published in JAMA Oncology, which statement is correct?

  1. A. The clone infiltrated the tumor in most patients
  2. B. After treatment of a solid tumor, CHIP was associated with more heart failure at ten years
  3. C. The association with arrhythmias remained significant after adjustment
  4. D. Chemotherapy was associated with a less frequent infiltrating clone
  5. E. A treatment specific to CHIP has proven cardiovascular efficacy
Show the answer

Answer B. Among 8,004 patients treated for a solid tumor, CHIP was associated with a higher 10-year incidence of heart failure; the association with arrhythmias was not significant. The clone infiltrated the tumor in 18.4% of patients, more often after cytotoxic chemotherapy, and no treatment specific to clonal hematopoiesis has proven cardiovascular efficacy.
